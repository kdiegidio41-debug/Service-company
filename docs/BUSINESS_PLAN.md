# Everglow Holiday Lighting Co. — Business Plan

*Operating plan for Seasons 1–3. Every number below is an assumption you should replace with your own market's data as soon as you have it — but they are realistic starting points based on how this industry actually prices and staffs.*

---

## 1. Executive summary

**The business.** Everglow designs, installs, maintains, removes, and stores professional Christmas light displays for residential and commercial properties. Customers pay one seasonal price. They never touch a ladder, a box, or a bulb.

**The model.** Sell in August–October. Install in October–December. Maintain through the season. Remove in January. Store until next November. Re-sell the same customer every year at a lower cost of acquisition and a higher margin.

**Why it's a good business:**

| Property | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| **~55–65% gross margin** | Labor and materials are the only real COGS. |
| **Cash up front** | 50% deposit at booking, balance on install. You're paid before January. |
| **60–75% annual renewal** | Because you're storing their lights, churn is structurally low. |
| **Year-2 margin jump** | Materials are already bought and amortized. Renewals are ~75% margin. |
| **Near-zero fixed overhead** | A trailer, a storage unit, and seasonal labor. No storefront. |
| **Cheap to test** | You can validate the whole model with $15–20k and one trailer. |

**Why it's a hard business:**

| Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| 90 days of revenue, 365 days of expenses | Pre-sell early, take deposits, and stack a shoulder-season service (see §9). |
| Weather compresses the install window | Book 20% under capacity; hold two float weeks in December. |
| Ladder/roof liability | Full GL + workers' comp before job one. Non-negotiable. Never skip it. |
| Labor is seasonal and untrained | Recruit in September, pay above market, rehire the same crew annually. |
| Everyone underprices year one | Price on value and takedown, not per foot against handymen. |

**Season 1 target:** 60 residential customers + 6 commercial accounts → **$126,000 revenue, ~$52,000 owner earnings.**

## 2. The offer

### What the customer actually buys

One price, one season, everything included:

1. **Design consult** — on-site or from photos + satellite. A rendering of their house lit.
2. **Materials** — commercial-grade C9/C7 LED, cut to fit their exact roofline. Everglow owns them; the customer never stores or replaces anything.
3. **Installation** — clips (never staples or nails), timers set, wiring hidden, everything tested at dusk.
4. **All-season maintenance** — the **24-Hour Fix**. Anything fails, we're back next day, free.
5. **Takedown** — by January 15, guaranteed.
6. **Storage** — labeled by address in our warehouse, ready to reinstall next year.

### Why "removal" is the whole strategy

Most operators sell installs and dread takedown. That's backwards. Takedown is:

- **The #1 stated customer fear.** "Who's going to get these down?" Answer it in the headline.
- **A moat.** Once you're storing a customer's custom-cut lights, switching to a competitor means re-buying everything.
- **The renewal engine.** In August you email: *"Your lights are labeled and on the shelf. Want the same design, or want to add the oak this year?"* That is a ~30-second sale.
- **A capacity filler.** January takedown keeps your crew employed and your trailer moving in a dead month.

So: **takedown and storage are included in every package, priced in, never an add-on, and stated in the first line of every ad.**

## 3. Market

**Target geography.** One metro, a 15-mile service radius, focused on 4–8 specific neighborhoods. Density is everything: two jobs on one street is a 40% efficiency gain over two jobs 20 minutes apart, and neighbors sell neighbors.

**How to pick your neighborhoods (do this in August with Zillow and a car):**

1. Median home value in the top 25% of your metro.
2. Homes built 1995–2020 — big rooflines, simple geometry, fast installs.
3. Drive them at 7pm in mid-December of the *prior* year. Count lit houses.
   - **A street with 30% of houses lit is the target.** Proof of category demand plus room to grow.
   - A street with 0% lit is a category education problem. Skip it.
   - A street with 90% lit is saturated by an incumbent. Skip it unless you can see their work is bad.
4. Cross-check HOA presence — HOAs create visible competition between neighbors and often buy entrance displays themselves.

**Customer segments:**

| Segment | % of revenue (target) | Avg ticket | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Residential — core | 60% | $1,400–$1,900 | Two-income, 35–65, 2,500–4,000 sq ft |
| Residential — estate | 15% | $3,000–$8,000 | Multi-story, wrapped trees, custom design. Highest margin. |
| Commercial | 25% | $3,500–$15,000 | Retail centers, HOA entrances, restaurants, dealerships, offices |

**Why commercial matters more than its revenue share suggests:** it books in **August–September**, before residential season starts. It fills your early calendar, funds your inventory buy, and it renews on contract. Target 20–25% of revenue from commercial by Season 2.

## 4. Pricing

### The model: price per linear foot, quote as a package

Internally you price by the foot. Externally you quote **one number for the season** so you're never compared like a commodity.

**Season 1 residential rates (first-year customer, Everglow supplies materials):**

| Line item | Rate | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Roofline, single story | **$5.00 / linear ft** | Includes lights, clips, install, service, takedown, storage |
| Roofline, second story / steep | **$6.50 / linear ft** | Risk and time premium |
| Tree wrap, trunk + branches | **$150–$400 / tree** | By height; a 25' oak is a 3-hour job |
| Shrub / bush | **$35 / shrub** | |
| Wreath, hung + lit | **$75–$150 ea.** | Customer's or ours |
| Garland | **$18 / linear ft** | |
| Pathway markers | **$12 / marker** | |
| **Minimum job** | **$850** | Hold this line. Small jobs destroy your day. |

**Renewal-year pricing:** **65–70% of year-one price.** The materials are already paid for. Tell the customer exactly why they're getting a discount — it makes the renewal feel earned and it makes your year-one price feel honest.

**Published package anchors** (what goes on the website):

| Package | Price from | Typical scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Roofline** | $895 | Front roofline, ~150 ft, timer, service, takedown, storage |
| **The Signature** | $1,650 | Roofline + entry + 2 trees + walkway. *Best seller.* |
| **The Estate** | $3,400 | Full property, multi-story, wrapped trees, custom design |
| **Commercial** | Custom | Multi-building, contract, scheduled maintenance |

> **Pricing discipline — the three rules that decide whether you make money:**
> 1. **Never quote per foot to a homeowner.** They will call three competitors and compare feet. Quote the season.
> 2. **Never discount to win.** Add value instead (a free wreath costs you $40; a 15% discount costs you $250).
> 3. **Never skip the minimum.** A $400 job takes 60% as long as a $1,600 job.

### Deposit and payment terms

- **50% deposit at booking.** This funds your inventory buy and it filters tire-kickers. Non-negotiable.
- **Balance due on install day**, collected on-site by the crew before they leave.
- Card, ACH, or check. Put a card on file for renewals.
- Commercial: 50/50 or net-15 on contract, with a signed MSA.

## 8. The operating calendar

This business is won or lost on the calendar. Print it.

| Month | The one job that month |
| --- | --- |
| **June–July** | **Buy inventory.** Lock pricing before the seasonal markup. Renew insurance. |
| **August** | **Sell commercial.** Property managers set budgets now. Email every prior-year customer for renewal — target 50% of renewals closed before Labor Day. Website and photos live and ready. |
| **September** | **Marketing on.** Ads live, SEO pages published, door hangers printed, crew hired and scheduled. Begin residential booking. |
| **October** | **Peak booking + start installs.** Commercial installs go first (they want lights by Nov 1). Residential design consults all month. Stop selling when you hit 90% of capacity — say "we're full, want the Season 2 list?" It's the best marketing you'll ever do. |
| **November** | **Install, install, install.** 70% of residential installs happen Nov 1–Dec 5. No selling; execution only. Photograph every job at blue hour. |
| **December** | **Finish installs by Dec 15. Then service only.** Run the 24-Hour Fix. Send thank-you cards and referral asks. Collect reviews *while the lights are on.* |
| **January** | **Takedown, Jan 2–15.** Inventory, label, and shelve every customer's kit by address. Replace failed strands now, not in November. |
| **February** | **Debrief.** What was the real cost per lead by channel? Which jobs lost money? Which crew was fastest? Rewrite pricing. Bank the cash. |
| **March–May** | **Offseason.** Optional revenue (§9). Book next season's inventory. Rest. |

## 9. Offseason revenue (optional but recommended)

Ninety days of revenue is a fragile business. The same trailer, ladders, crew, and customer list support:

| Add-on | Season | Why it fits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Gutter cleaning** | Spring + Fall | Same ladders, same roofs, same customers, same skill. The single best fit. |
| **Window & solar panel washing** | Spring–Summer | Same access equipment, high margin, recurring. |
| **Permanent architectural lighting** (track-mounted year-round LED) | All year | $4k–$12k tickets. Sell it to your best Christmas customers as an upgrade. Fastest-growing segment in this trade. |
| **Landscape / patio lighting** | Spring–Summer | Same wiring skills, same design conversation. |
| **Event & wedding lighting** | Spring–Fall | Venue relationships, weekend work. |

Sell these to the list you already own. A Christmas customer who trusts you on their roof in December will hire you for their gutters in April.

## 10. Operations

**Crew.** Two people per crew: one installer on the ladder, one groundhand cutting, testing, and stabilizing. Pay above the local handyman rate — you are asking them to work at height in the cold, and rehiring the same crew every year is worth far more than the wage difference. Recruit in September from roofing, landscaping, and window-cleaning crews entering their offseason.

**Safety — this is the thing that ends companies.**
- General liability ($2M) + workers' comp before the first job. No exceptions, no "I'll get it after a few jobs."
- Ladder stabilizers/standoffs on every ladder. Never lean on a gutter.
- Harness and rope on any pitch over 6/12 or any second story.
- No installs in rain, ice, or sustained wind over 20mph. Reschedule. A day's revenue is not worth a fall.
- Document a 10-minute daily safety brief. Photograph the ladder setup at each job. This is what your insurer will ask for.

**Materials standard.**
- Commercial-grade C9 LED on SPT-1/SPT-2 wire, cut and assembled to length per house. Never retail pre-made strands.
- All-clips, no staples, no nails. Damage to a customer's fascia is the fastest path to a refund and a one-star review.
- Every kit tested before it leaves the shop, and again at dusk on install day.
- Label every customer kit with address + zone on removal. A labeled kit turns a 3.5-hour reinstall into a 2-hour one.

**Software stack (keep it cheap and boring):**

| Need | Option |
| --- | --- |
| CRM + scheduling + invoicing | Jobber or Housecall Pro (~$100–200/mo, seasonal) |
| Quote form → CRM | The website form in this repo, wired to your form endpoint |
| Payments | Stripe or the CRM's built-in processing |
| Measurement | Google Earth Pro (free) — measure rooflines from satellite before the visit |
| Reviews | Automated SMS review request 3 days post-install |
| Design mockups | Photo of the house + light lines drawn in any photo app. Close rate goes up ~15% with a visual. |

**The install-day checklist (give this to every crew):**
1. Confirm with homeowner on arrival. Walk the plan.
2. Photo the fascia and gutters *before* touching them.
3. Set ladder with stabilizer. Groundhand foots it.
4. Install roofline → trees → shrubs → wreaths → pathway.
5. Hide every cord. Set the timer (dusk to 11pm is the default).
6. Test at dusk or with a tester. Every socket.
7. Walk the finished job with the homeowner. Collect the balance.
8. Blue-hour photo from the street. Yard sign in.
9. Text the photo to the customer that night. **This is what generates the review and the referral.**

## 11. The metrics that matter

Track these five weekly during season. Ignore everything else.

| Metric | Season 1 target | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Leads per week** (Sep–Nov) | 12+ | The top of everything. |
| **Quote-to-close rate** | 40%+ | Below 30% means your price is wrong or you're quoting too slowly. |
| **Time to first response** | Under 15 minutes | The first company to respond wins ~50% of the time. This is free and most competitors are bad at it. |
| **Average ticket** | $1,550+ | If it's falling, you're discounting or taking small jobs. |
| **Renewal rate** (measured in August) | 65%+ | The whole business. |

Two more, checked once at season end: **cost per acquired customer** by channel (kill anything over $250) and **gross margin per job** (find and fire the job types that lose money).

## 12. Risks and honest failure modes

| Risk | Reality | Response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **You underprice Season 1** | Most new operators do, out of fear. | Hold the $850 minimum and the published package floor. Losing a cheap customer is a win. |
| **Weather eats two weeks** | It will happen. | Book to 85% of capacity. Hold Dec 8–15 as float. Never promise a specific date in November — promise a week. |
| **Injury** | A fall ends the company and possibly a life. | Insurance + harness + no-bad-weather rule. Enforce it even when you're behind. |
| **You sell more than you can install** | The most common way this business fails publicly. | Cap bookings. A waitlist builds status; a missed install builds one-star reviews. |
| **Renewals don't renew** | Usually caused by a bad takedown or a slow service call. | The 24-Hour Fix and a clean January are your retention program. |
| **Cash crunch in September** | Inventory is bought before revenue arrives. | 50% deposits + commercial contracts closed in August. |
| **A competitor undercuts you 30%** | They will. | Don't match. They are not insured, not storing lights, and will not come back in January. Say that, calmly, in the quote. |
