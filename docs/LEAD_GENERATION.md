# Everglow — Lead Generation Playbook

*How to fill a 60-job calendar from a standing start, in priority order. Do them in this order — the top of the list is cheaper and faster than the bottom.*

---

## The one thing to understand first

**This market has a 10-week buying window.** Nobody thinks about Christmas lights in June, and nobody books on December 15. Search volume for "christmas light installation" is near zero all year, then spikes ~40x from mid-September, peaks the first week of November, and collapses after December 5.

That means:

- **Everything must be live and indexed by September 1.** Website, Google Business Profile, ads, review base. If you launch in October, you've already lost half the season.
- **Ad budgets should be front-loaded to Sep 15 – Nov 20.** Spending in December is spending on people who won't book.
- **Speed of response beats everything.** These buyers are contacting 3 companies in one sitting. The first to respond wins roughly half the time. A 15-minute response is a competitive advantage that costs you nothing.

---

## Tier 1 — Free, highest ROI. Do these first.

### 1. Google Business Profile (your single most important asset)

For local service searches, the GBP map pack outranks everything, including your website. Treat it as the primary storefront.

- Claim and fully verify it. Primary category: **"Holiday lighting service."** Secondary: "Lighting contractor," "Christmas store."
- Set a **service area** (your neighborhoods), not a street address, unless you have a real shop.
- Upload **20+ blue-hour photos** of real jobs. Photos are the #1 conversion lever in the map pack. Add new ones weekly during season.
- Fill every service field: *Christmas light installation, Christmas light removal, holiday lighting design, commercial holiday lighting, light storage.*
- Post weekly from September through December (Google Posts). A photo and two sentences is enough.
- Turn on messaging and answer within minutes.
- **Reviews are the ranking factor.** See #2.

**Target: 25 reviews before your second season.** That alone will usually put you in the top 3 of the map pack in a suburban market.

### 2. A review engine, run relentlessly

- Ask **while the lights are on and the customer is delighted** — the night of the install, not in January.
- The crew texts the blue-hour photo that evening with: *"Here's your house tonight. If you're happy with it, a quick Google review helps us more than anything."* Include the direct review link.
- Follow up once, 3 days later, if no review. Then stop.
- Respond to every review, including the bad ones, within 24 hours. Publicly fixing a complaint sells better than a 5-star review.
- Expect ~30–40% conversion with this script. 60 jobs → ~20 reviews in one season.

### 3. Yard signs at every job

- 18×24, Midnight ground, Bulb mark, **"Lights by Everglow"** + phone + short URL.
- Ask permission during the walkthrough: *"Mind if we leave a small sign by the driveway through the season? We'll pull it at takedown."* ~70% say yes.
- Expect **~1 lead per 6–8 signs**, and those leads close at a very high rate because they've seen your actual work on their own street.

### 4. Neighbor door hangers ("we just lit up your neighbor's house")

The highest-ROI paper you will ever print. After every install, hang 8–10 doors on both sides and directly across.

> **We just lit up your neighbor's house.**
> We're on your street this week. Rooflines, trees, wreaths — installed, maintained all season, taken down in January, and stored until next year. You never touch a ladder.
> **Same-street pricing through Friday.** Text a photo of your house to (267) 853-0058 for a quote in 24 hours.
> — Everglow Holiday Lighting Co.

Density is the point: two jobs on one street cuts drive time and doubles your visibility.

### 5. The referral program

- **$100 credit off next season for the referrer, $100 off the first job for the referred.** Credit, not cash — it locks in a renewal.
- Give every customer two physical referral cards with the thank-you note.
- Mention it exactly twice: at the install walkthrough and in the January takedown email.
- Referrals close at 60–70% and cost you nothing until they convert.

### 6. Your own network, worked deliberately

Season 1 specifically: your first 10 customers should come from people who already know you. Post the before/after photos on your personal social accounts. Text 30 people directly — not a mass post — with: *"Started a Christmas lighting company. Doing 60 houses this year. Want me to do yours?"* This is unglamorous and it is how nearly every one of these companies got its first ten jobs.

## Tier 2 — Paid, high intent. Turn on September 1.

### 7. Google Local Services Ads (LSA)

**Start here before regular search ads.** LSA sits above everything, is pay-per-lead not per-click, and carries the "Google Guaranteed" badge — which matters enormously to someone letting a stranger on their roof.

- Requires background check + license/insurance verification. **Start this in July; it takes weeks.**
- Budget: $1,200–$2,000 for the season.
- Expect **$25–$60 per lead**, closing at 30–45%. That's a $70–$180 acquisition cost against a $891 gross profit — the best paid channel in the trade.
- Dispute junk leads. Google credits them, and most operators never bother.

### 8. Google Search Ads

- Budget: $2,000–$3,500, weighted Sep 15 – Nov 20.
- Exact/phrase match only. Keywords that convert:
  `christmas light installation near me`, `christmas light installers [city]`, `holiday light installation [city]`, `christmas light removal`, `christmas light company [city]`, `commercial christmas lighting`
- **Negative keywords, day one:** `diy`, `how to`, `walmart`, `home depot`, `lowes`, `for sale`, `buy`, `cheap`, `wholesale`, `jobs`, `hiring`, `salary`, `show`, `display near me`, `drive through`, `zoo`, `lights show`
- Send traffic to `/quote.html`, never the homepage. The dedicated quote page converts roughly 2x better.
- Add call extensions and turn on call-only ads for mobile 4–8pm.
- Expect $6–$14 per click, 8–15% form conversion, **$70–$140 per lead.**

### 9. Meta (Facebook/Instagram) ads

Different job than Google: Google captures demand, Meta *creates* it — someone scrolling who hadn't thought about it yet.

- Budget: $1,500–$2,500, Sep 15 – Nov 15.
- **The creative that works is the before/after blue-hour pair.** Daylight house, then lit house. Nothing else comes close. Video walk-up beats static.
- Targeting: 3–5 mile radius around your target neighborhoods, homeowners, 35–65, HHI top 25%.
- Best-performing angles, in order:
  1. *"You'll never climb a ladder again."*
  2. *"We take them down in January and store them for you."*
  3. *"Your neighbors on [Street Name] already booked."*
- Retarget everyone who visits the site and doesn't submit the quote form. Cheapest conversions you'll buy all season.
- Expect **$18–$45 per lead**, lower intent than Google, so respond even faster.

### 10. Nextdoor

Underrated for this trade — hyper-local, homeowners, and the whole product is neighbors recommending services.

- Claim a free business page. Post before/afters (as the business, not sock accounts).
- Get real customers to recommend you in their own neighborhood feed. Ask directly.
- Paid local ads are cheap relative to Meta in most markets. Budget $500–$1,000.
- Do not spam. One self-promotional post per neighborhood per month, maximum.

## Tier 3 — Commercial. Highest ticket, longest lead time. Start in July.

Commercial is 25% of your revenue and it books before residential season starts, which is exactly when you need cash for inventory.

**Who to call (in order of how easy they are to close):**

1. **Restaurants and bars** — owner decides on the spot, wants it done by Thanksgiving.
2. **HOA boards** — entrance monuments and common areas. Boards meet monthly and set budgets in Aug/Sep. Get on an agenda.
3. **Retail strip centers and property managers** — one manager can own 8 properties. The single highest-leverage relationship in this business.
4. **Car dealerships** — big budgets, love visibility, want it up early.
5. **Office parks, banks, medical plazas, senior living** — steady, contract-friendly, renew without a fight.
6. **Municipalities and downtown business districts** — the biggest tickets and the slowest process. These are RFPs with a 6–12 month cycle. Start the conversation this year for next year.

**The commercial approach:**

- Go in person in **July and August**, before anyone is thinking about it. A folder with 6 blue-hour photos, proof of $2M insurance, and one page of pricing.
- Lead with **liability and reliability**, not beauty: *"Your maintenance guy shouldn't be on a 24-foot ladder over your parking lot. We're insured for $2M and we're back within 24 hours if anything goes out."*
- Offer a **3-year contract at a locked rate.** Property managers love a decision they don't have to remake annually.
- Ask every commercial client for the names of their peers. Property managers all know each other.

## Tier 4 — SEO and content. Slow, compounding, start now for next season.

You will not rank in Season 1. Publish anyway, because Season 2 is nearly free traffic.

**Build these pages by September 1** (one per target neighborhood/suburb):

- `/christmas-light-installation-[city]`
- `/christmas-light-removal-[city]`
- `/commercial-christmas-lighting-[city]`
- `/holiday-lighting-cost-[city]` — **a real pricing page.** Almost no competitor publishes prices. The page that answers "how much does this cost" captures enormous search volume and pre-qualifies every lead who reads it.

Each page needs: real photos from that area, the neighborhood named in the H1, an FAQ block with schema markup, and a quote form above the fold.

**Blog posts that actually earn links and traffic:**
- "What professional Christmas light installation costs in [City] in 2026"
- "C9 vs C7 vs mini lights: what actually looks good on a roofline"
- "Why you shouldn't staple lights to your fascia"
- "When should you book Christmas light installation?" (answer: September)

**Technical basics:** schema markup for `LocalBusiness` + `Service` + `FAQPage`, fast load, mobile-first, NAP (name/address/phone) identical across every directory. List on Yelp, Angi, Thumbtack, Bing Places, Apple Maps, and Nextdoor — consistency matters more than volume.

## The follow-up system (where most competitors lose)

Getting the lead is the easy half. Most operators in this trade are genuinely bad at follow-up — that's your opening.

| When | Action |
| --- | --- |
| **Within 15 minutes** | Text + call. Even at 9pm. Even if you can't quote yet: *"Got your request for [address] — I'll have a number to you tomorrow morning."* |
| **Within 24 hours** | Deliver the quote. Include a blue-hour photo of a comparable home. Quote a package price, never per foot. |
| **Day 2** | Text follow-up: *"Any questions on the quote? I'm holding a spot the week of Nov 10 — can hold it 48 hours."* |
| **Day 5** | Call. Ask directly what's holding them back, then answer it. |
| **Day 10** | Last touch: *"Booking up for the season — want me to keep you on the list or close this out?"* Then stop. |
| **August, next year** | Re-contact every unclosed lead from last season. They're warm, they remember you, and it costs nothing. |

Two scripts worth memorizing:

**"You're more expensive than the other guy."**
> "We probably are. Three things that price covers: we're insured for $2 million if anything happens on your roof, we come back within 24 hours all season if a strand goes out, and we take everything down in January and store it — so you're not doing this again next year. The cheaper quote usually isn't including two of those three."

**"I'll think about it."**
> "Totally fair. One thing worth knowing: I'm booking [Week of Nov 10] right now and I'll be full by mid-October. Want me to hold that week for 48 hours, no obligation, while you decide?"

## The renewal campaign (the most profitable email you'll send)

Every August, one email to every prior customer. This is a ~$1,150 sale for about 30 seconds of work.

> **Subject: Your lights are on the shelf, [Name]**
>
> Hi [Name] — your lights from last season are labeled, tested, and sitting on our warehouse shelf ready to go back up.
>
> Same design as last year is **$1,150** — 30% off first-year pricing, since the materials are already yours.
>
> Want to add anything this year? A lot of folks add the big oak out front or wreaths on the second-story windows.
>
> Just reply "same as last year" and I'll put you on the November schedule. First week of November is still open.
>
> — Kris, Everglow

**Target: 50% of renewals closed before Labor Day.** Follow up once in early September, once in early October. Then call the holdouts — a personal call recovers roughly a third of them.
