*Version 1.0 — the single source of truth for how Everglow looks, sounds, and behaves.*

## 1. The name

# EVERGLOW
### Holiday Lighting Co.

**Why this name works:**

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| Says what we do | "Glow" = lights. "Holiday Lighting Co." carries the category. |
| Memorable | One word, three syllables, no spelling ambiguity on a phone call. |
| Ownable | Not a description ("Bright Lights Inc."), so it can be trademarked and defended. |
| Evocative | *Evergreen* + *glow* — pine trees and warm bulbs in one word. |
| Scalable | Not locked to a city or a founder's last name. Works if you franchise or sell. |
| Sayable | "Everglow, this is Kris." Clean on the phone, clean on a voicemail. |
| Domain-friendly | `everglowlighting.com`, `everglowholiday.com`, `goeverglow.com` |

**Legal/registration checklist before you print anything:**

1. Search the USPTO free database (`tmsearch.uspto.gov`) for "Everglow" in Class 037 (installation services) and Class 011 (lighting).
2. Search your state's business entity registry for name conflicts.
3. Buy the `.com` plus defensive `.net`, and the matching handles: `@everglowlighting` on Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, YouTube.
4. Register the LLC, then file a DBA only if the LLC name differs.
5. File the trademark (~$350/class) once you have revenue — you can operate on common-law rights until then.

**Backup names, if the domain or trademark is blocked** (all vetted for the same criteria):

1. **Northglow** — colder, more premium, great for northern markets.
2. **Lumen & Pine** — upmarket, design-forward, best for high-end estate work.
3. **Bright Season Co.** — friendliest, most family-brand.
4. **The Ladder Co.** — cheeky, leans on the #1 customer pain point (nobody wants to climb).
5. **Twelve Nights Lighting** — literary, memorable, distinct in a sea of "Bright/Merry/Shine" names.

## 2. The positioning

> **Everglow is the full-service holiday lighting company for homeowners who want a professionally designed, perfectly installed display — and never want to think about it again.**

We are **not** a light-hanging service. We are a **season-long subscription to a beautiful house.**

**The one-sentence pitch (use this everywhere):**

> "We design it, we install it, we maintain it all season, we take it down in January, and we store it in our warehouse until next year — you never touch a ladder."

**The strategic wedge — takedown and storage.**
Every competitor sells the install. Half of them treat takedown as an upsell and none of them want to do it. Takedown is where customers get abandoned. We lead with it:

- Takedown is **included in every package**, never an add-on.
- Storage is **included** — their garage stays empty, their lights stay untangled.
- The takedown promise is what makes renewal automatic: we already have their lights.

**Who we're for (and who we're not):**

| We are for | We are not for |
| --- | --- |
| Homeowners with a 2,000+ sq ft home who value their weekends | Bargain shoppers pricing per-foot against a handyman |
| Two-income households, ages 35–65 | DIYers who enjoy the project |
| HOA neighborhoods with visible competition between houses | Anyone who wants to supply their own Walmart lights |
| Commercial: retail centers, restaurants, HOA entrances, car dealers, offices | One-off "just do this one tree" calls under our minimum |

**Positioning against the three competitor types:**

| Competitor | Their weakness | Our line |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The handyman with a ladder | No insurance, no design, no warranty, disappears in January | "Licensed, insured, and we come back — guaranteed." |
| The national franchise | Rotating crews, rigid packages, upsell scripts | "Same owner-led crew every visit. Designed for your house, not a catalog." |
| DIY / retail lights | 6 hours on a ladder, tangled boxes, blown fuses, roof damage | "Your Saturday back, and a display that actually looks professional." |

## 3. The promise (our four guarantees)

These four lines go on the website, the truck, the yard sign, and the estimate. They are the brand.

1. **The No-Ladder Promise** — You never climb. Not to install, not to fix, not to take down.
2. **The 24-Hour Fix** — A bulb, strand, or timer fails, we're back within 24 hours. No charge, all season.
3. **The Clean Slate** — Takedown by January 15 and free warehouse storage, included in every package.
4. **The Same-Crew Guarantee** — Owner-led, background-checked, uniformed, insured. The same faces every year.

## 4. Voice & tone

**Everglow sounds like a very good contractor who happens to have great taste.**
Warm, plainspoken, specific, and completely un-desperate. We never beg for the job.

| We say | We never say |
| --- | --- |
| "We handle it." | "Let us take the hassle out of the holidays!" |
| "Commercial-grade LEDs, cut to fit your roofline." | "Premium quality lights!" |
| "Takedown is included. Always." | "Ask about our takedown add-on!" |
| "Most homes land between $1,400 and $2,600." | "Affordable pricing for every budget!" |
| "We're booking November now." | "LIMITED TIME OFFER — ACT NOW!!!" |

**Rules:**
- Numbers over adjectives. "24-hour fix" beats "fast service."
- Short sentences. A ninth grader reads them without stopping.
- Never use "hassle-free," "one-stop shop," "unleash," "elevate," or more than one exclamation point per page.
- Talk about *their* house and *their* January, not our company's mission.
- Warmth comes from specifics and confidence, not from exclamation marks or emoji.

## 5. Color

The palette is **a winter night, lit from within.** Dark ground, warm light. It's what a great display actually looks like at 6pm.

| Role | Name | Hex | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Primary ground | **Midnight** | `#0B1220` | Page backgrounds, hero, footer |
| Ground raised | **Deep Night** | `#111C2F` | Cards, panels on dark |
| Signature accent | **Bulb** | `#FFC46B` | The glow. Buttons, highlights, the logo bulb |
| Accent light | **Warm White** | `#FFE7C2` | Glow halos, hover states |
| Secondary | **Pine** | `#14513C` | Evergreen support, badges, illustration |
| Secondary light | **Frost Green** | `#3E8F6E` | Success states, checkmarks |
| Alert / festive | **Cranberry** | `#C2413C` | Sparingly — urgency, sale banners, one accent per page |
| Light ground | **Snow** | `#F7F9FC` | Light-mode sections, forms |
| Text on dark | **Snow Text** | `#EAF0F7` | Body copy on Midnight |
| Muted text | **Slate** | `#93A2B8` | Captions, labels, meta |

**Rules:**
- Bulb (`#FFC46B`) is the brand. Use it for exactly one thing per screen: the action you want taken.
- Never put Cranberry and Bulb next to each other at the same size — the eye can't pick a winner.
- The dark:light ratio across the site should be roughly 70:30. Dark sells the glow; light sells the trust (pricing, forms, FAQ).
- Never use pure black (`#000`) or pure white (`#FFF`) as a large field.

## 6. Typography

| Role | Typeface | Fallback stack | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Display / headlines | **Fraunces** (Google Fonts), optical size 144, weight 600 | `Georgia, 'Times New Roman', serif` | Warm, slightly old-fashioned serif. Reads as "craft," not "corporate." |
| Body / UI | **Inter** (Google Fonts), 400/500/600 | `-apple-system, 'Segoe UI', Roboto, sans-serif` | Clean, neutral, high legibility at small sizes. |
| Numerals in pricing | Inter, weight 600, tabular figures | — | Prices must align in columns. |

**Rules:**
- Headline sizes scale fluidly with `clamp()`. Never below 15px for body copy.
- Line length caps at ~68 characters for paragraphs.
- Letter-spacing: `-0.02em` on display, `0` on body, `+0.14em` uppercase on small labels.
- Never use a script/"Christmas" font. It's the single fastest way to look cheap and to become unreadable at a distance.

## 7. The logo

**Primary wordmark:** `EVERGL(O)W` — set in Fraunces, uppercase, with the **O replaced by a lit bulb**: a filled circle in Bulb amber with a soft radial glow and a small filament stem on top.

**Lockups:**
- **Primary** — wordmark + "HOLIDAY LIGHTING CO." in Inter, letterspaced, centered beneath.
- **Horizontal** — bulb mark, then wordmark, then rule, then "HOLIDAY LIGHTING CO." Use in the site header and email signature.
- **Mark only** — the glowing bulb in a circle. Use for the favicon, social avatar, hard hat sticker, and yard-sign corner.

**Clear space:** the height of the bulb on all four sides. Never crowd it.

**Minimum sizes:** wordmark 120px wide on screen / 1.25in in print. Mark alone: 24px / 0.35in.

**Never:**
- Recolor the bulb anything but Bulb amber or solid Snow (knockout on dark).
- Add a drop shadow, bevel, outline, or gradient to the wordmark.
- Set the logo on a busy photograph without a scrim.
- Stretch, rotate, or arch the wordmark.
- Replace the bulb with a snowflake, star, or Santa hat. Ever.

Assets live in `assets/img/` (`logo.svg`, `logo-mark.svg`, `favicon.svg`).

## 8. Photography & imagery

**The rule: shoot at blue hour, never at night.**
Blue hour is the ~25 minutes after sunset when the sky is deep blue but still holds detail. Lights read as brilliant, the house is still visible, and the photo looks expensive. Full-dark photos look like black rectangles with dots.

- Every install gets a **before (daylight)** and **after (blue hour)** shot from the same spot. That pairing is our highest-converting asset — it works on the site, in ads, and in the estimate PDF.
- Shoot wide enough to include the driveway and a tree. Context sells scale.
- Include one human element in ~1 in 5 photos: a crew member on a ladder, a family on the porch. People make it feel like a service, not a stock photo.
- **Never** use stock photography of lights on the website. Customers can smell it, and it undercuts the entire trust argument.
- Get a signed one-line photo release in the service agreement so you can use every house you touch.
