# Building your lead list — legally, and in about two hours

---

## 1. Why we are not scraping homeowners

Scraped residential lists exist to be cold-called and cold-texted, and that is the part
that will end you.

**A single marketing text to a cell phone without prior express written consent is
$500 — $1,500 if a court calls it willful.** There is no harm requirement: one
un-consented text is a complete cause of action, and consumers can sue directly. It is a
class-action industry. One campaign to 1,000 scraped numbers is **$500,000 of exposure**
before anyone argues about willfulness, and Colony Ridge settled a text case just under
$2 million in 2025. As of January 2026 consent must also be obtained by *your* business
specifically — you cannot buy it, share it, or inherit it from a list vendor who claims
their numbers are "opted in."

Pennsylvania stacks its own telemarketer registration and Do Not Call rules on top.

You are selling a premium, trust-led service to Chestnut Hill and Blue Bell. The channel
that gets you sued is also the channel that gets you screenshotted into a neighborhood
Facebook group. Not worth it.

**The QR code on your sign is the correct version of this.** Someone scans it and texts
you first — that is inbound, fully consented, and no TCPA issue exists. Same for LSA
calls, Nextdoor, and your quote form.

## 2. Residential, done legally

| Channel | Legal footing | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Direct mail** | No TCPA. Mail is not a call. | You **can** legally buy a targeted mailing list — filter by ZIP, home value $600k+, owner-occupied, 2,000+ sq ft. Brokers: Accurate Append, Data Axle, Melissa. ~$0.08–0.15/record. This is the legitimate version of "getting me a list." |
| **Door hangers** | No consent needed. Don't put them *in* mailboxes — that's a federal mail violation. | Your highest-ROI paper. 8–10 doors either side of every install. |
| **Google LSA + Search** | Inbound. | Turn on now. Highest intent traffic you will get. |
| **Nextdoor / local Facebook groups** | Inbound, but read group rules. | Post as a neighbor, not an ad. |
| **Door knocking** | Legal in most townships; some require a solicitation permit. | Call the township first — it's a 5-minute call. |

**Direct mail is the answer to what you were actually asking for.** Buy the list, mail a
blue-hour postcard in mid-October, put the sign QR on it. Legal, targeted, no exposure.

## 3. Commercial — do this one **now**

It is mid-September. Your own plan says commercial books in August–September and funds
your inventory buy. This is the list to build this week.

Business contact information is public and B2B outreach does not carry the residential
restrictions. This is the part you can legitimately go compile.

### Build the list in one sitting

Open Google Maps and run these searches, set to your 15-mile radius. Export each into
the tracker. Twenty minutes per category, and you'll have 150+ names.

```
restaurants in Ambler PA
restaurants in Chestnut Hill Philadelphia PA
shopping center in Plymouth Meeting PA
property management company in Montgomery County PA
car dealership near Willow Grove PA
bank branch in Blue Bell PA
medical office building in Fort Washington PA
senior living in Dresher PA
country club in Whitemarsh PA
garden center in Montgomery County PA
funeral home in Glenside PA
hotel in Fort Washington PA
```

For HOA boards, search the Montgomery County Recorder of Deeds for the association name,
or look for the management company's sign at the development entrance — that sign is the
lead.

The mall management offices are worth one call each even if they say no — they manage
other properties, and that referral is the whole prize.

### The walk-in

Go in person, 2–4pm on a Tuesday through Thursday. Not Monday, not Friday, not during a
lunch rush. Bring a folder: six blue-hour photos, your certificate of insurance, one page
of pricing.

> "Hi — I'm Kris with Everglow, we do holiday lighting here in the area. I'm booking
> commercial installs for this season. Two things people care about: your maintenance guy
> shouldn't be on a 24-foot ladder over the parking lot, and if a section goes dark in
> December we're back within 24 hours. Takedown in January is included. Can I leave a
> folder and follow up Thursday?"

Then **actually follow up Thursday.** That is where the job is won — most competitors
never do.

Ask every single one: *"Who else manages property around here that I should talk to?"*
Property managers all know each other, and that one question compounds faster than
anything else you can do.
