# Product shortlist, 18 November 2026

Two `product-scout` runs (everyday problem-solvers, and hobby gear), about 80 searches,
then spot checks on the top picks. **No candidate passed every filter on verified
evidence.** The demand is real in each case below; the supplier cost is not verified for
any of them, and that is the number that decides whether a product works with paid ads.

Next step for all three: a real landed-cost quote (product plus US shipping) from
CJdropshipping, Zendrop or AliExpress, then a sample order. The research container
could not reach those sites, Reddit, or Exploding Topics.

---

## 1. Hands-free needlepoint stand for stretcher bars (best market)

An adjustable lap or table stand whose clamp fits needlepoint stretcher bars, so the
stitcher has both hands free and can flip the canvas to check the back.

**Why it has potential**
- Needlepoint is the fastest-growing hobby the scouts found:
  - Gen Z searches for needlepoint bags were up 550% year over year ([Axios, Apr 2026](https://www.axios.com/local/austin/2026/04/10/austin-needlepoint-popularity-grows)).
  - NYC shops report beginner kits selling out within days ([YPulse, Jan 2026](https://www.ypulse.com/newsfeed/2026/01/20/needlepoint-is-the-latest-granny-hobby-gen-z-cant-put-down/)).
- A clear price gap:
  - Specialty stands sold through needlepoint shops are expensive. The System 4 is $60-195 for the stand ([Penny Linn](https://pennylinn.com/products/needlework-system-4-lap-table-stand-with-radius-arm)), and its stretcher-bar clamp is sold separately ([Stitching Fox](https://www.stitchingfox.com/collections/system-4-stands/products/lap-table-stand-stitching-fox)).
  - Cheap Amazon and Walmart stands ($22-49) are built for embroidery hoops, and retailers warn their clamps don't fit some frames.
  - Nobody sells a modern, affordable stand aimed at the new needlepoint crowd.
- Indoor winter hobby: a January launch lands in its peak season.

**Economics (estimates, not verified)**
- Landed cost: about $12-18.
- Price: $59-69.
- At $69 with an $18 landed cost: about $48 gross profit, a break-even CPA of about $48, and a break-even ROAS of about 1.4.

**What could kill it**
- **Hard to advertise.** There's no big "wow" moment, so this one needs organic
  stitch-with-me content more than cold paid ads.
- **Clamp quality and fit.** A clamp that doesn't grip stretcher bars means returns. The
  sample must be tested with real bars.
- **Copying the System 4 or Lowery design.** Their patents are unverified; source a
  different design.
- **Box size and weight:** medium.

## 2. Electric yarn winder + swift "hank to cake" set (best video)

A cordless winder plus a yarn swift, sold as a set: it turns a twisted hank of yarn into a
center-pull cake in about a minute.

**Why it has potential**
- Crochet and knitting demand is verified:
  - Searches for analog crafts were up 136% in six months, and yarn accessory sales were up 40% year over year (Michaels 2026 Creativity Trend Report, reported by [AOL](https://www.aol.com/lifestyle/8-biggest-crafting-trends-2026-194606392.html)).
  - The top Amazon winders sell about 2,000 units a month ([ASINSIGHT](https://www.asinsight.com/report/US/caydo-electric-yarn-winder), a third-party estimate).
- The yarn cake winding up is a naturally satisfying video.
- Same buyer as #1, which opens the door to a fiber-arts accessories brand rather than
  a one-product store.

**Economics (estimates, not verified)**
- Landed cost: about $25-32 for the set.
- Price: $69-79.
- At $74 with a $30 landed cost: about $41 gross profit, and a break-even ROAS of about 1.8.

**What could kill it**
- **It's already crowded.** Caydo runs its own store ([caydo.com](https://www.caydo.com/collections/electric-yarn-winder)), Amazon generics sell for $27-45, and the cordless Etcokei is $59.99. The set has to look clearly better than a $27 winder.
- **Quality.** Reviews of generics mention squeaking, units dying after three months, and
  weak tension arms.
- **Shipping the battery.** The lithium battery in cordless units complicates air shipping.

## 3. Premium gold panning starter kit (best hook, wrong season)

A curated kit: two pans with riffles, a classifier, a snuffer bottle, vials, tweezers, a
magnifier, and a backpack. Positioned as a modern "weekend prospector" kit.

**Why it has potential**
- Gold passed $4,500 an ounce, with news coverage of Americans taking up panning
  ([IBTimes, about May 2026](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/modern-gold-rush-americans-prospecting-1806009)).
- The best three-second hook of anything found: the sand washes out and gold flakes
  glint in the pan.
- The Amazon price zone is $20-50, and there's room above it for a premium kit
  ([ASINSIGHT](https://www.asinsight.com/report/US/gold-panning-kit)).

**Economics (estimates, not verified)**
- Landed cost: about $15-25 (bulky box).
- Price: $59.
- About $32-42 gross profit.

**What could kill it**
- **The season.** River panning is a summer activity. Launch it in April, not now.
- **The gold price.** Demand depends on it, and a pullback cools interest.
- **Established brands.** Garrett, Minelab and SE hold Amazon, and China-shipped kits are
  already on Fruugo ([example](https://www.fruugo.us/gold-panning-kit-15-inch-pan-for-efficient-gold-particle-capture-and-river-prospecting/p-458767764)).
- **Ad policy.** No "make money" or income claims in ads.

# Round 2: mass-market products (same day)

The owner ruled out niche hobbies and asked for products with mass appeal. I checked the
broadest demand signals available: Google's 2026 Holiday 100, Exploding Topics' November
2026 product list, and Amazon sales estimates. **Every broad product with strong demand
already has a cheap seller dominating it.** That's the trade-off with mass appeal.

| Product | Demand signal | Why it fails or struggles |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mini rice cooker | Searches up 438% to 33,100 a month ([Exploding Topics](https://explodingtopics.com/topic/mini-rice-cooker)) | Dash Mini sells for $24.99 with about 47,000 reviews ([ASINSIGHT](https://www.asinsight.com/report/US/dash-mini-rice-cooker)). It's also a heated electrical appliance that needs UL/ETL certification. |
| Magnetic fridge/washer shelf | Up 983% on Exploding Topics ([product topics](https://explodingtopics.com/product-topics)) | The top Amazon spice rack sells about 10,000 a month at a $24 average ([ASINSIGHT](https://www.asinsight.com/report/US/magnetic-spice-rack-for-refrigerator)), and some sell for $5. |
| Portable laptop monitor | Up 838% on Exploding Topics | Amazon's top 10 average about $66-73 ([ASINSIGHT](https://www.asinsight.com/report/US/portable-computer-monitor-for-laptop)), which leaves no margin. |
| Lymphatic face brush | Up 400% on Google's Holiday 100 | Sells for $7-20, and selling it means implying "drainage" and "sculpting" health claims. |
| Sunrise alarm clock | No rising data found | Hatch, Philips and Echo hold Amazon. |

## The two broad products worth considering

### A. Portable wireless CarPlay / Android Auto screen

A dash-mounted touchscreen that gives a car without CarPlay or Android Auto maps,
music and calls from the phone.

- **Demand:** the "CarPlay screen for car" category averaged about 14,500 units and $1.2M
  a month across its top 50 Amazon listings in the year to July 2026
  ([ASINSIGHT](https://www.asinsight.com/market-analysis/US/carplay-screen-for-car)).
  Many TikTok Shop sellers carry it.
- **Competition:** heavy. The top portable unit sells about 10,000 a month, and the top 10
  average $64.49 ([ASINSIGHT, May 2026](https://www.asinsight.com/report/US/portable-carplay)).
  You'd win on ads and offer, not on being first.
- **Economics (estimate, not verified):**
  - Sell at $89-99, with a landed cost of about $30-40.
  - That leaves about $48 gross profit per order at $89, and a break-even ROAS of about 1.85.
- **Video:** an old car's dash with a phone stuck in a vent mount, then the same car with
  a big touchscreen showing maps. Easy to understand in two seconds.
- **Risks:**
  - "CarPlay" is Apple's trademark. Say "works with Apple CarPlay," never use Apple's
    logo, and note that most generic units aren't Apple-licensed.
  - Electronics defects and returns.
  - Reviews complain about mounts that won't stick and laggy navigation.

### B. Japanese floor mattress (tri-fold or roll-up)

- **Demand:** the fastest-growing broad trend in the data. Searches rose from 3,837 to
  22,200 a month ([Exploding Topics](https://explodingtopics.com/product-topics)). It's used
  for small apartments, guest beds, kids' rooms, dorms and the minimalist-room look.
- **Competition:** no dominant listing found.
  - Walmart sells it for $51-68, and MAXYOYO sells for about $67.
  - FULI sells about 100 a month at $299 ([ASINSIGHT](https://www.asinsight.com/report/US/japanese-mattress)).
  - There's room for a mid-tier version at $129-169.
- **The problem is size.** A single tri-fold weighs about 8 kg and folds to about
  140 x 66 x 30 cm ([Superdelivery](https://www.superdelivery.com/en/r/pd_p/13143858)).
  - Shipping that from China wipes out the margin.
  - It only works with a supplier that holds stock in a US warehouse.
  - Returns are close to impossible, so expect to refund without taking the item back.

## Recommendation

Test before building. For a mass-market product, the store with the best video wins, not
the store that found the product first. Order one CarPlay screen sample, film 3-5 short
videos, and post them organically on TikTok and Reels. Build the store and fund ads only
if a video gets real traction, such as comments asking where to buy.

crowded. Checked and rejected:

## Best option: a plastic-free kitchen starter kit (compete on the offer, not the product)

Each piece on its own is a commodity. A curated kit, sold as "swap the plastic that
touches your food," is a different offer, and a big retailer has already proven it.

- **Demand:**
  - Microplastics gets about 90,000 searches a month ([Exploding Topics](https://explodingtopics.com/topic/microplastics)).
  - "Plastic-free coffee maker" searches rose 5,000% in 2026 ([Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/home/coffee-makers/sugar-creamer-and-microplastics-the-inside-story-on-the-plastic-free-coffee-revolution)).
  - Mainstream food outlets ran plastic-free kitchen pieces in June 2026, and the US government funded a $144M microplastics program in April 2026.
- **Competition:**
  - Grove Collaborative sells a 12-piece "Kitchen Swap Set" at $125.90, shown out of stock ([Grove](https://www.grove.co/products/highest-impact-kitchen-swap-set-everyday-food-contact-essentials)).
  - Elephant Box in the UK shows its kit sold out. Seed & Sprout covers Australia and New Zealand.
  - No US brand running ads for a plastic-free kit was found. That's unverified: check the Meta Ad Library.
- **Kit (target 2 lb or under):** nesting stainless containers with stainless lids, a
  reusable stainless coffee filter, wooden utensils, plus one or two more food-contact
  swaps.
- **Economics (estimate, not verified):**
  - Factory cost is about $12-20.
  - Add about 35% duty and roughly $7-12 for DDP shipping, for a landed cost of about $23-39.
  - At $69, that leaves about $28-44 gross profit, and a break-even ROAS of about 1.6-2.5.
  - Margin is the weak spot, so get real CJ quotes first. It improves when the kit moves to bulk stock in a US warehouse.
- **Claims:**
  - Say what's factually true: "no plastic touches your food: stainless steel, wood and
    silicone seals."
  - Don't claim health benefits.
  - Don't call silicone plastic-free without saying it's silicone.
- **Brand path:** start with the one hero kit, then add a plastic-free coffee kit and a
  steel-lid bottle. That's the "store around it" idea, in a big market.

# Round 4: products for women (light, non-electric, no clothing sizes, nothing applied to skin)

## Best option: a "host a charm bar at home" party kit

Everything needed for 6-8 guests to build their own bag charms or necklaces:
- 60-80 charms
- chains, clasps and jump rings
- two pairs of pliers
- a display tray

**Demand**
- Exploding Topics shows "bag charms" at about 246,000 searches a month (+358%) and
  "charm bar" at 22,200 (+510%) ([fashion topics](https://explodingtopics.com/fashion-topics)).
- In-store charm bars draw lines and sell out
  ([JCK](https://www.jckonline.com/editorial-article/charm-jewelry-trend/)).
- Brands run charm bars as event activations
  ([Event Marketer](https://www.eventmarketer.com/article/experiential-marketing-trend-of-the-week-charm-bars/)).
- They've gone viral for bachelorette parties
  ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/local/charlotte/2024/10/07/charm-necklace-bracelet-holst-lee-jewelry-charlotte)).

**The gap:** today an at-home charm bar means booking a mobile service, at about $25 per person,
or a $150 setup fee plus about $20 per person
([Batch listings](https://letsbatch.com/experiences/9679a57b-1239-4229-85d8-6c2b2cf8de2c)).
The kits on sale are bead kits, not charm bars: BaubleBar ($35-58), MoMA ($48), and
Mack & Rex bracelet kits ($44.99 each). No self-serve charm-bar party kit was found.
That's from one search, so check Amazon, Etsy and TikTok Shop for "charm bar kit."

**Economics (estimate, not verified)**
- Charms cost about $0.10-1.00 each wholesale
  ([Alibaba](https://www.alibaba.com/product-introduction/Enamel-Flower-Charms-Bulk-Spring-Charm_1601101632964.html)).
- The kit costs about $10-20 from the factory. Add about 35% duty and DDP shipping for 1-1.5 lb,
  for a landed cost of about $20-35.
- Sold at $89, that leaves about $50-65 gross profit and a break-even ROAS of about 1.4-1.8.
- Refill packs of 30-40 charms at about $29 bring buyers back.

**Who buys and when**
- Bachelorette parties and bridal showers
- Birthdays and girls' nights
- Galentine's Day (Feb 13)
- Holiday gifting

**Risks**
- **The trend can peak.** Move fast.
- **Gold plating on brass tarnishes within weeks.** That's the main complaint risk. Use
  stainless steel findings and tarnish-resistant plating, and describe the materials truthfully.
- **Metal safety.** Get lead and cadmium test reports from the supplier. Market it to adults and
  teens, never to children under 12; children's jewelry has strict federal rules.
- **Character charms.** No Sanrio, Disney or Labubu-style charms. They're common in 1688 catalogs
  and are trademark infringement.
